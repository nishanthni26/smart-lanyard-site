# Smart Lanyard Content

## When to use this skill
Use whenever writing or revising Smart Lanyard website copy, landing pages, FAQs, school or workplace outreach, product explainers, social posts, or SEO content.

## Brand and audience
Smart Lanyard is a connected e-paper ID card and platform concept for schools, campuses, workplaces, events, and organisations that need identity, approved access, attendance, live updates, and safety support in one familiar card experience. Write for school leaders, administrators, HR and operations teams, campus managers, parents, and decision-makers evaluating a pilot or demonstration.

Every piece should make the product easier to understand, build practical trust, and move the reader toward requesting a demo at https://smartlanyard.in/demo.

## Voice and tone
- Sound professional, calm, modern, and useful rather than futuristic for its own sake.
- Use plain Indian English. Prefer “organisation”, “programme”, and “recognise” where natural.
- Use active voice and short paragraphs. Explain technical terms on first use.
- Lead with the reader’s operational problem and outcome, then explain the feature.
- Do not overclaim safety, security, attendance accuracy, battery life, compliance, integrations, or production readiness.
- Describe SOS as a configured alert workflow that helps trusted teams respond; never promise emergency response or guaranteed outcomes.
- Describe QR, NFC, e-paper, dashboards, and updates as intended capabilities unless the specific implementation is confirmed.
- Do not publish unverified prices, timelines, certifications, customer logos, school names, statistics, or competitor comparisons as facts.

## Formatting and SEO
- Use one clear H1. Use H2 for major topics and H3 for supporting sections; never skip heading levels.
- Keep paragraphs to 2–4 sentences and write for scanning.
- For SEO pages, choose one primary keyword and 1–2 supporting phrases. Put the primary keyword in the title, first paragraph, and one H2 naturally.
- Target keyword themes include: connected ID card for schools, digital student ID card, NFC attendance, school safety alerts, e-paper ID card, workplace digital ID, and campus access.
- Keep title tags near 50–60 characters and meta descriptions near 120–160 characters.
- Add descriptive alt text to useful product images; use empty alt text for decorative marks.
- Link naturally to https://smartlanyard.in/, https://smartlanyard.in/faq, and https://smartlanyard.in/demo.
- Use accurate canonical, Open Graph, X/Twitter, and JSON-LD metadata when editing pages.

## Default content structure
1. Specific headline with the reader’s problem or outcome.
2. Short introduction that names the audience and primary keyword.
3. Practical sections describing the workflow, feature, implementation considerations, and limits.
4. Clear takeaway without inflated claims.
5. A single call to action: “Request a Smart Lanyard demo” linking to https://smartlanyard.in/demo.

## Research and sourcing
Research time-sensitive claims against official or primary sources. Date prices, regulations, product availability, technical specifications, and market statistics. If a claim cannot be verified, remove it or label it as a proposal, estimate, or planned capability.

## Worked example
> **Connected ID cards for schools, without reprinting every update**
>
> A school ID card should help people recognise a student, confirm approved access, and find the right information at the right moment. Smart Lanyard is designed to keep identity details, passes, updates, and configured safety alerts together in one familiar card experience. It is a starting point for a clearer school-day workflow, not a replacement for trusted staff decisions.

- [ ] Primary keyword appears naturally in the title, opening, and one heading.
- [ ] Claims are labelled as confirmed, proposed, or estimated where needed.
- [ ] Images have useful alt text and lazy loading when offscreen.
- [ ] CTA links to the demo route and is not repeated excessively.
